A benchmark for System One decision models

SO-Arena

Benchmarking the Capability Spectrum and Reliability of System One Models

Lichen Li*, Ziping Dong*, Sicheng Feng, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang†

* Equal contribution  ·  † Corresponding author

Paper coming soon Code 🤗 Dataset See the leaderboard ↓

System One models promise fast, general-purpose decision-making without lengthy token generation. SO-Arena characterizes their capability spectrum and decision reliability across 11 domains and five capability families, with verified labels, difficulty tiers, interactive sandboxes and two reliability tests.

    Why SO-Arena

    More than an aggregate score

    Understanding System One models needs more than aggregate accuracy on existing benchmarks. Four design choices let SO-Arena show where a model is strong, where it breaks down, and whether its decisions can be relied on.

    Decision reliability

    Does the decision hold when the options change?

    Accuracy on one fixed option set overlooks whether a decision holds when the options change. SO-Arena changes the options in two controlled ways and combines both tests into one reliability score.

    S Order stability

    Each item is shown in its original order and in 12 reorderings. S is the share of reorderings in which the model keeps its answer, identified by content, rescaled so that random picks score 0 and full agreement scores 1.

    N Null discrimination

    Each item is shown as it is and with its correct option removed. N = 2·AUC − 1 measures how well the model's probability that no option is correct separates the two: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect.

    R Reliability

    The geometric mean R = √(S·N), positive only when both exceed chance, reported as a percentage next to the capability score.

    Findings

    What 21 models tell us

    Where System One models match System Two, and what closes the gap

    Score difference from GPT-6 Luna on the medium and hard tiers, with 95% confidence intervals; the gain from a larger, newer backbone; and the gain of StartLux Auto from always thinking over never thinking.

    Backbone gain: the average gain from the smaller to the 27B model of the four families released in both sizes (every smaller model builds on Qwen3.5, every larger one on Qwen3.8-27B).

    Inside the benchmark

    2,919 tasks, 11 domains, five capability families

    Tasks are built from 40 established sources, checked by people, organized into domains and difficulty tiers, and posed as one decision interface: static questions and environment-based tasks in web and tool sandboxes.

      How the leaderboard is scored

      The original task is the unit of scoring, and domains are weighted equally, so that larger domains do not dominate. Each domain contributes its hardest tier (Math: hard and very hard; untiered domains: all tasks), which makes the score more sensitive to differences on demanding tasks: 1,232 tasks in all.
